# Generated Story Engagement Contract

This contract applies to future Story AI output. It does not require the deterministic provider-free closed-beta fallback to become the final product-quality story experience.

The deterministic fallback exists first as a safe, reliable demo and recovery path. When Story AI is deliberately enabled later, generated stories must be noticeably more engaging than that fallback while preserving the same safety, privacy, localization, memory and cost boundaries.

## What the child should feel

For ages 5–7, bedtime does not mean uneventful. The story should be calm enough to read before sleep, but it should still make the child want to know what happens next.

A strong story should quickly create one of these forms of curiosity:
- a funny or surprising event;
- a small mystery or question;
- a clear child-scale desire;
- a friend who needs help;
- an object, creature or place behaving in an unexpected but safe way.

The story should then keep paying that curiosity forward through action, dialogue, discovery and consequence until the ending slows down.

## Opening hook

For 5–7 bedtime stories:
- hook the child within roughly the first 30–50 words;
- make the main desire/problem understandable within roughly the first 100 words;
- do not spend the opening paragraph mainly describing scenery;
- prefer dialogue, a visible action, a small surprise or an immediate question/problem.

A warm atmosphere may appear immediately, but atmosphere must support the scene rather than delay it.

## Description budget

Description is useful when it helps the child picture something that matters to the current action.

Avoid:
- three or more consecutive sentences of static scenery or explanation;
- lists of decorative details that do not affect what happens;
- repeatedly restating that the place is calm, beautiful, quiet, safe or magical;
- long narrator explanations of what a character feels when a visible reaction could show it instead.

Prefer one or two concrete sensory details, then move the scene forward.

## Scene momentum

Every one or two short paragraphs should contain a meaningful change. For example, somebody:
- acts;
- reacts;
- discovers something;
- asks or answers;
- makes a joke;
- tries something;
- makes a small mistake;
- notices a clue;
- decides what to do next.

The change does not need to be loud or exciting. A bedtime story can stay gentle while still moving.

## Character and dialogue

Dialogue and visible action should carry much of the story. Supporting characters should have distinct behavior rather than existing only to explain the lesson.

Use small, memorable traits such as:
- a character who always miscounts;
- a careful character paired with an impulsive one;
- a tiny character attempting an oversized task;
- a recurring joke or harmless habit that matters to the plot.

Do not turn the hero into an adult supervisor, project manager or moral lecturer.

## Anticipation and payoff

Keep one simple anticipation loop alive through the story. The child should have a reason to continue listening, such as wanting to know:
- whether the funny plan will work;
- what made the unusual sound;
- where the strange light leads;
- how a friend will solve a small problem;
- what will happen after the child's selected choice.

Pay that loop off before the final bedtime coda. Do not replace the payoff with a lecture or a generic statement that everyone learned something.

## Choice consequence

The child choice should alter visible events after the choice. Two choices may still reach the same broad safe ending, but the route, interactions, discoveries or remembered artifact should feel meaningfully different.

The bridge after confirmation stays short. Episode 2 continues after the bridge and spends most of its time showing the consequence rather than repeating the selected action.

## Bedtime ending

The final 10–15% may deliberately reduce energy. By then the main question or problem should already be solved.

The closing section can use softer sensory detail, shorter dialogue and a warm final image. Calmness belongs especially here, after the child has already received the story payoff.

## Release rule

Word-count, duration, safety and structural checks are necessary but cannot prove that a story is interesting. Before Story AI is enabled for real users, generated samples must also pass human editorial review for:
- hook strength;
- description density;
- scene momentum;
- child curiosity;
- dialogue/character life;
- meaningful choice consequence;
- natural RU/UZ language;
- satisfying but calm closure.
