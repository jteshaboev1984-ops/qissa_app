## 1. Exactly what the owner has approved

The owner accepted the conservative solution to the difference between Samira's burgundy main travel clothing and her **already approved cream/teal appearance at S2E5's fork**.

**Approved interpretation:** Samira can wear a *removable light cream travel over-tunic/robe* over her established **burgundy riding base**, with a *removable teal travel cloak/long drape*. The approved fork illustration depicts this **outer travel ensemble**; the burgundy underneath is largely hidden from the selected camera angle. It is **not** a permanent replacement for her principal costume, not proof she bought an entirely new wardrobe, and not a story event requiring a paragraph about doing laundry.

At Rustam's road post, **after both roads converge and before the group leaves toward the tower**, the travel over-tunic and teal cloak **can be taken off and stowed**, revealing her main burgundy riding clothes for the continuing same-day mission. The text expressly provides a regrouping/preparation interval of **half an hour**: «— Выезжаем вместе через полчаса». That makes the visual transition plausible; **the action of removing garments is an editor-approved staging inference, not a described action in the prose**. No extra choice outcome or narrative explanation may be invented.

This rule is **narrow**. It does not grant permission for unlimited changes of outfit; only practical layers, minor cleaning/repair during documented home or overnight stops, and new specific styles after future owner approval.

## 2. The locked recognizable base

### Temur — teal rider base

- About twelve; lean Central Asian boy; warm medium skin, short dark hair, expressive face, no adult proportions.
- Primary: deep teal knee-length Central Asian riding chapan/tunic with narrow muted gold edging, light beige trousers, dark brown riding boots, brown leather belt and small practical satchel.
- In approved outdoor visual references, a **small patterned blue cap** often appears. It is the normal outdoor look for new travel pictures, but **not a hard requirement in every interior/night/rest scene** and it must not be presented as a newly discovered literary fact. Keep hairstyle and face recognizable if the cap is removed.
- When the text establishes official Rider duty, add the wide Rider's belt and manageable **sheathed** curved sabre; never treat these as permanent nightwear.
- A practical removable travel cloak/scarf is allowed. During the **plateau choice only**, high wind can make Temur's cloak flap because the branch text specifically supports it.
- After the Festival's forest escape, World Bible V2 demands a torn and dusty right sleeve *until he changes clothes after that story*. Do **not** carry that damage automatically into Season 2, where ordinary weeks and home life have passed.
- In the owner-approved **S2E7 morning horses** frame, warmer inner fabric and a blue outer drape are visible. Treat this as permitted **overnight re-layering**, not as a newly approved permanent replacement for his teal primary identity.

### Samira — burgundy rider base + accepted removable cream/teal travel over-ensemble

- About twelve; lean Central Asian girl; warm medium skin; **one long, thick, dark braid** (can be mildly loosened after the road); no adult makeup.
- Primary: burgundy/maroon knee-length Central Asian riding chapan/tunic with muted gold edging, beige trousers, dark riding boots, brown leather belt and small satchel. A delicate circlet is present in some approved images; **not obligatory in all contexts**, no heavy crown.
- Optional S2E5 travel outer ensemble (already visible in the accepted fork): a loose **cream over-tunic** of believable manually woven material plus **teal draped cloak**; cut to allow riding, leg movement, reins and realistic folding. The burgundy base is underneath but not necessarily visible. Do not accidentally draw an implausibly bulky double robe; use thin outer fabric.
- **Forbidden visual interpretation:** cream/teal is suddenly Samira's mandatory full-time main outfit, or the cream robe appears without any transition **after** the post scene where the story's existing later images return to burgundy.
- During late Festival scenes Samira's hem/knees/side are muddy after the landslide; this damage is **not** permanently carried through weeks later into Season 2.

### The horses NEVER change because a costume changed

- **Shamol → Temur**: dark bay nearly black, black mane/tail, **small narrow white forehead star**, no white socks, deep teal saddle blanket.
- **Bulut → Samira**: light **dapple-gray** with darker gray mane/tail, **no** forehead star/blaze, deep burgundy saddle blanket.
- Both remain athletic young horses with sensible tack and scale. Never swap horses, tack, saddlebags or character ages; do not confuse a pale cloak with Bulut's pale coat.

## 5. Acceptance checklist for every *new* illustration

Record **before** generating (use the JSON ledger's `editorial_preflight_fields`): episode/part/phase, exact RU+UZ anchor, location and time, branch/knowledge state, characters present, primary outfit, removable layers, accumulated dirt/damage, most recent confirmed home/rest, correct horse/tack, adjacent approved frame, visual spoilers and owner review state.

Reject if any of these materially fail:
1. A character appears before/after the scene contrary to the actual text.
2. Samira's E5 cream/teal look is mistaken for a permanent new base or abruptly reappears after the post without a new reason.
3. A complete outfit is suddenly replaced mid-ride or mid-fight without a break.
4. The same-day dirt/weathering mysteriously vanishes; conversely Festival mud or torn right sleeve persists weeks without justification.
5. Wrong person/horse assignment, forehead markings, colors, anatomy, headwear as a compulsory helmet, or modern fittings.
6. An image reveals a hidden route result, clue, leader, criminal network sign or identity too early.
7. The image includes text that requires separate RU/UZ translation without an approved localized production plan.
8. Facial proportions or poses turn children into young adults or the rider does not plausibly control the horse.
