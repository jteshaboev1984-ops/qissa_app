> Scope: story continuity + **provisional visual design draft** for future stories and illustrations

## 1. Why this file exists

«Праздник мужества» is the first approved story in this world. Future stories, prompts, illustrations, covers, episode previews and memory records must use the same established facts.

The literary text should stay readable and should not repeat full appearance descriptions every time. Detailed recurring visual information belongs here and in character/location reference assets.

There are three levels:

- **HARD CANON** — established and must remain consistent.
- **VISUAL CANON V1** — locked design details for illustrations once approved.
- **OPEN / NOT YET CANON** — intentionally undecided; do not invent a permanent answer inside a later story without an explicit canon update.

# 2. World identity

## HARD CANON

**World name:** Королевство семи дорог.

**Known concept:** seven important roads connect the kingdom. The annual Festival of Courage takes place in the capital.

**Period / technology:** pre-industrial storybook world based on horses, roads, handwritten letters, wax seals, sabres, guards, road posts and walled cities. No modern vehicles, firearms, phones, electricity, modern signage or contemporary clothing.

**Magic status:** no magic has been established in V1. Future stories must not casually treat magic, magical creatures or supernatural powers as already normal parts of the world. If magic is ever introduced, that is a separate canon decision.

**Cultural / architectural direction:** Central Asian / Silk Road-inspired fantasy rather than generic medieval Western Europe. It is a fictional kingdom, not a direct reconstruction of one historical state.

Use visual language such as:
- broad courtyards;
- arched gates;
- carved wood;
- stone and warm plaster walls;
- restrained glazed-tile accents;
- textiles and riding clothes inspired by Central Asian silhouettes;
- practical horse tack and travel gear.

Avoid turning the world into:
- a European Gothic castle setting;
- a modern city;
- an Arabian-fantasy cliché;
- a direct copy of a real historical dynasty.

# 3. Story 1 — fixed chronology

The following events are HARD CANON.

1. The Festival of Courage happens once a year.
2. Sixteen children take part in the selection shown in V1.
3. Temur and Samira did not know each other before that day.
4. The selection includes running, archery and preparing a horse for travel.
5. Temur and Samira become the two young royal couriers for the final challenge.
6. Both receive identical sealed royal letters.
7. Both must ride to the same city — Lugovoy — give the letter to its ruler, receive a separate sealed reply and return it to the king.
8. The stated rule is that the winner is the one who first **honestly** completes the mission and returns the reply.
9. Temur receives Shamol; Samira receives Bulut.
10. Both receive a road book.
11. A fallen tree blocks the main road.
12. Samira knows from the road book that the upper path is open and shorter.
13. Samira lies to Temur: «Через лес. Эта закрыта.»
14. Temur trusts her and rides through the Eastern Forest.
15. Samira takes the shorter upper route.
16. In the Eastern Forest Temur notices the bandits' sign on a tree.
17. Bandits block his route and demand his horse and bag.
18. The same sign appears on one bandit's leather bag.
19. Temur uses his short sabre to cut a rope blocking the passage. He does **not** attack the bandits.
20. Temur escapes because of quick thinking, riding skill and Shamol.
21. Shamol jumps a ditch during the escape.
22. After the escape Temur and Shamol have established real mutual trust.
23. Samira reaches a river after the previous night's rain.
24. She uses the road book to recognize that the lower ford is unsafe and finds the old stone crossing upstream.
25. Samira reaches Lugovoy first.
26. The ruler of Lugovoy records that Samira arrived first and that her royal seal was intact.
27. Temur reaches Lugovoy later.
28. Temur tells the ruler and city guard about the forest bandits and reproduces their sign from memory.
29. Lugovoy's guard says other travelers have previously mentioned people carrying the same mark, but the attacks had not yet been clearly connected.
30. The ruler includes this information in Temur's sealed reply to the king.
31. Samira leaves Lugovoy first and expects to win.
32. On the return route she discovers a rain-damaged landslide/road collapse hidden beyond a bend.
33. She realizes Temur may approach too fast to see the danger.
34. Samira gives up the chance to preserve her lead and stays to warn him.
35. She climbs higher so Temur can see her before the bend.
36. Wet ground collapses beneath her and she ends up on a ledge below the road.
37. Bulut remains safely above and neighs.
38. Temur sees/hears Bulut, stops in time and learns Samira is below.
39. Temur does not attempt a reckless solo rescue. He rides to the nearby road post and brings adults and proper ropes.
40. Samira is rescued.
41. Afterward Samira voluntarily admits that the upper route at the first fork had been open and that she lied to Temur because she wanted to win.
42. Temur does not instantly erase the wrongdoing. He says he is glad she is alive but will still be angry.
43. Samira asks why Temur did not simply ride on.
44. Temur answers: «Тогда я бы всё равно знал, что уехал.»
45. They return to the capital almost together.
46. Both reply seals are intact.
47. The king reads Samira's reply first and confirms that she reached Lugovoy first.
48. Temur's reply alerts the crown to the bandit pattern and sign.
49. Samira tells the full truth about the first fork herself.
50. The king makes clear that the Festival tests more than speed.
51. Samira is the first courier to reach Lugovoy.
52. Both Temur and Samira are judged worthy to become young royal knights.
53. The bandit problem is **not solved** in Story 1.
54. By the end of Story 1 the king and captain of the royal guard know the bandits' sign.
55. The bandits and their sign are an unresolved hook for later stories.

Do not later rewrite these facts as if:
- Temur won the race outright;
- Samira never lied;
- Temur defeated the bandits in combat;
- the bandits were captured;
- the two children were old friends before the festival;
- they rode to different cities;
- there was only one letter/reply;
- the king already knew everything about the bandits before Temur's report.

# 11. Visual continuity rules for all illustrations

These rules are mandatory once final art production starts.

### A. Never rely on prose prompt alone for recurring characters

Each recurring person/horse needs one approved reference sheet. Future illustrations should use:
- the reference image itself where the image system supports references;
- the exact asset ID;
- the locked visual description from this file.

A new prompt must not freely redesign a recurring character.

### B. Stable identifiers

For every recurring visual asset, preserve:
- face shape;
- age;
- skin tone;
- hair color and hairstyle;
- eye color;
- body proportions;
- base outfit silhouette;
- horse coat and markings;
- tack;
- recurring prop design;
- gang symbol geometry.

### C. Outfit continuity inside one story

During the same journey:
- Temur stays in the teal riding outfit;
- Samira stays in the burgundy riding outfit;
- Shamol keeps teal saddle cloth;
- Bulut keeps burgundy saddle cloth.

Do not change costume color because an image model produced a prettier result.

### D. Damage continuity

Illustrations must remember events:
- before Eastern Forest escape: Temur's outfit is intact;
- after escape: right sleeve/forearm is torn/dusty;
- before landslide: Samira's outfit is relatively clean;
- after rescue: mud/damp-earth marks remain through the return and ceremony.

### E. Prop continuity

If a character has the letter case or road book in one sequence, its appearance must match earlier images.

The road book is not magically larger, differently colored or re-bound later.

### F. Left/right continuity

If a fixed physical feature or object placement is introduced in a reference sheet, do not mirror it randomly in later art.

Important example:
- the bandit crescent opens right;
- its slash rises lower-left → upper-right.

### G. Scene continuity

The same location must retain major anchors across images:
- palace shape and steps;
- city gate;
- road direction;
- tree/forest visual family;
- Lugovoy gate/civic hall.

Camera angle may change. Architecture identity may not.

### H. Age safety / child depiction

Temur and Samira must always visibly read as children around 12, not as 16–20-year-old fantasy heroes.

No sexualized clothing, poses or adult glamour treatment.

### I. Violence depiction

Story tension may be shown, but illustrations should prefer:
- pursuit;
- blocked road;
- rope cutting;
- horse jump;
- rescue;
over explicit weapon-on-person violence.

Temur's sabre scene should visually show him cutting the rope, not attacking a bandit.

### J. Art style lock

The approved master style reference is **QISSA_MASTER_REFERENCE_user_selected.png**, previously selected by the owner. Working style label: **seven_roads_painterly_v1**. The master image is the authoritative style reference; descriptive text is secondary.

Before producing a full set of final story illustrations:
1. choose one approved master style;
2. create reference sheets for Temur, Samira, Shamol, Bulut, King and key props;
3. approve one capital establishing shot and one outdoor road/forest shot;
4. lock that style version;
5. produce the remaining images from those references.

Do not mix watercolor, 3D animation, photorealism and comic-book rendering in the same story unless a later explicit art-direction decision says so.
